CONE RUSH.
Roblox UI design.

Built for the rush: a bright, playful interface for a Roblox party game where traffic cones are the objective, balls are the obstacle, and the next round is never far away.

Role
Game UI & icon design
Target platforms
Roblox · PC, phone & tablet
Scope
Loading · HUD · 3 shop tabs
Icon system
8 icons & buttons
Reference canvas
1920 × 1080

The brief

Dodge the ball.
Bring back the cone.

CONE RUSH combines dodgeball with capture-the-flag. Runners collect traffic cones and carry them back to the start line while Catchers throw balls to knock them down. Between rounds, players can explore collectible balls, trails and a currency built around a gold traffic cone.

The client wanted a high-quality, cartoony interface for kids and teens: bright, glossy and bubbly, with a personality of its own. The brief called for a loading screen, a lobby HUD, a reusable shop window with three tabs, and eight icons or buttons.

My design direction brings those pieces together with cone-yellow highlights, orange active states, cobalt outlines and a soft sky backdrop. Rounded shapes and bold labels carry the playful tone across every view.

The design challenge

Keep the energy.
Give every action a place.

01 / Identity

Build from the game’s own objects.

Traffic cones, balls and clouds give the interface its visual starting point. The cone becomes both a brand cue and the currency symbol, tying the menus back to play.

02 / Hierarchy

Let the middle breathe.

The lobby groups navigation at the sides and progression along the bottom. Round information sits above the play area, with the central space left open.

03 / Consistency

Make the shop feel connected.

One frame, three tabs and a shared card-and-button language give the crate, gamepasses and coin packs a recognizable home.

02 / Lobby HUD

A clear centre.
A lively frame.

Trails, Shop and Balls form a consistent left-hand stack. Starter Pack, Deluxe Pack and Spectate sit opposite, while the cone balance and buy-coins shortcut stay together at the lower left.

The countdown, waiting-player count and next-map label share the top-centre area. A level badge and XP bar anchor the bottom, separating long-term progression from the next round’s information.

03 / The shop system

Three tabs.
One familiar window.

The shop keeps its title, balance, close control and tab positions in the same place. Orange marks the selected tab; cobalt outlines and light panels define the content underneath.

Green purchase buttons share a common shape and treatment. Reusing the frame, tabs, cards and buttons gives the developer a visual basis for matching future windows.

These presentation views show the visual layout. Crate odds, gamepass benefit copy, pass artwork and purchased/owned states need to be populated or connected during implementation. Displayed balances and prices are sample screen content.

04 / Icons & buttons

Small pieces.
Plenty of character.

Shop, Trails, Balls, Spectate, Starter Pack and Deluxe Pack each have an illustrated identity, supported by the cone-currency symbol and buy-coins shortcut.

Chunky borders, rounded frames, bright colour fields and outlined labels connect the set. The same assets appear in the HUD so the navigation and the supporting icon board tell the same visual story.

From design to development

A system the game can grow with.

The brief uses a 1920 × 1080 reference canvas and calls for phone layouts around 2.2:1, plus tablets. The visual system provides a starting point for that implementation, with a few responsibilities to carry into Roblox Studio.

  1. Keep the loading pieces independent.

    The logo, background, bar track and fill need separate implementation layers so loading progress can animate while the percentage and status update.

  2. Scale the groups, then check the safe areas.

    Side controls and the XP bar need layout rules for each viewport. Reserve the top area requested for Roblox controls, allow for phone notches and check comfortable tap targets on devices.

  3. Reuse components and connect real data.

    Carry the common frame, tab, card and button treatment into other windows. Connect balances, drop chances, benefits and ownership states to the game’s systems.

The design outcome

A party-game identity,
across every view.

The loading screen, lobby and shop now share a clear visual direction: cone-inspired accents, confident blue outlines, soft panels and expressive icons. Each view has its own purpose while the repeated components keep the set connected.

The project brings together five interface views and an eight-part icon/button set, with a shared visual language for loading, finding the next action and exploring the shop.
