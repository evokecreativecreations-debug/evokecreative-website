Logo vs Brand Identity for Startups

Decide whether your startup needs a logo or a full visual identity. Compare the handoff, launch assets and Evoke Creative’s starting packages.

By Rameez Ahmed · November 11, 2026

A startup logo gives customers a mark to recognize. A brand identity adds a system of colors, typography, supporting marks and usage rules. Choose a logo when you need a few basic launch assets. Choose a visual identity when several people need to build a website, pitch deck, social posts or packaging that look connected.

When is a logo enough for launch?

A logo can be enough for a simple landing page, a social profile and an email signature. Agree on those uses before approving a concept. Ask to see the wordmark in the actual header space, a small mark at favicon size if one is in scope, and a one-color version on both light and dark backgrounds.

The Logo Foundation package starts at $250 USD and includes one custom direction, two revision rounds, and SVG, transparent PNG and JPG exports. Extra concepts, secondary marks and wider applications are quoted separately. If you need both a wordmark and a distinct icon, put both in the brief.

When does a startup need a brand identity?

Choose an identity when different people will create materials independently. A web designer needs type and color choices, a founder needs a deck that feels related, and a print vendor needs usable files. A concise guide gives those people a shared set of decisions.

The Brand Foundation package starts at $750 USD. It includes a primary logo and supporting variation, a palette, typography recommendations, a concise PDF guide and two revision rounds. List extra applications such as stationery or social templates separately when you request a quote.

What does a fuller identity look like in practice?

In the JacyJo Photography identity redesign, an editorial wordmark is supported by secondary marks, type, color and a pattern. The business card and price list show how those decisions carry across materials. If you only need a landing page now, prioritize the assets that will appear there. If you are preparing multiple pieces for launch, ask to see how the system works across them.

What should I include in my logo design brief?

  1. List the first three places your logo will appear and give the designer their real dimensions.
  2. Share the audience, brand name, any required tagline and references with a sentence about each one.
  3. Ask which logo variants, editable files and revision rounds the quote covers.
  4. Review the mark on the launch materials before approving the handoff.

Compare starting prices and deliverables, or share your launch brief if you need a quote for a specific mix of assets.
